# Hiragana (ひらがな) — Complete Guide

Hiragana is the first script to master. It is used for native Japanese words, grammatical endings (okurigana), particles, and furigana (reading aids over kanji). Target: read every character in under 1 second and write all of them from memory within 3–4 days.

---

## 1. The 46 Basic Characters (五十音 gojūon)

Read rows left → right (a, i, u, e, o). Columns are the consonant rows.

| Row | a | i | u | e | o |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| — (vowels) | あ a | い i | う u | え e | お o |
| K | か ka | き ki | く ku | け ke | こ ko |
| S | さ sa | し **shi** | す su | せ se | そ so |
| T | た ta | ち **chi** | つ **tsu** | て te | と to |
| N | な na | に ni | ぬ nu | ね ne | の no |
| H | は ha | ひ hi | ふ **fu** | へ he | ほ ho |
| M | ま ma | み mi | む mu | め me | も mo |
| Y | や ya | — | ゆ yu | — | よ yo |
| R | ら ra | り ri | る ru | れ re | ろ ro |
| W | わ wa | — | — | — | を **wo** (pronounced *o*) |
| N (syllabic) | ん n | | | | |

Irregular romaji: し = shi (not si), ち = chi (not ti), つ = tsu (not tu), ふ = fu (not hu), を = wo/o.

**Particle pronunciation rules (very important for reading):**
- は as the topic particle is pronounced **wa** (わたし**は** = watashi **wa**).
- へ as the direction particle is pronounced **e** (とうきょう**へ** = tōkyō **e**).
- を is only used as the object particle and is pronounced **o**.

## 2. Dakuten (゛) and Handakuten (゜)

Two small strokes (゛ dakuten) voice the consonant; a small circle (゜ handakuten) turns h → p.

| Base row | a | i | u | e | o |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K → G | が ga | ぎ gi | ぐ gu | げ ge | ご go |
| S → Z | ざ za | じ **ji** | ず zu | ぜ ze | ぞ zo |
| T → D | だ da | ぢ **ji** (rare) | づ **zu** (rare) | で de | ど do |
| H → B | ば ba | び bi | ぶ bu | べ be | ぼ bo |
| H → P (handakuten) | ぱ pa | ぴ pi | ぷ pu | ぺ pe | ぽ po |

Total: 20 dakuten + 5 handakuten = 25 characters.

Notes:
- ぢ and づ sound identical to じ and ず. They appear almost only in compound words (はな**ぢ** nosebleed, つ**づ**く to continue) or after つ/ち doubling (ちぢむ to shrink).
- じ is used in nearly all other cases (じかん time, じしょ dictionary).

## 3. Yōon (拗音) — Contracted Sounds

Take an i-row character (き, し, ち, に, ひ, み, り, ぎ, じ, ぢ, び, ぴ) and add a **small** や, ゆ, or よ (ゃ ゅ ょ). The small kana is written at roughly half size, in the lower-right (horizontal writing) or lower-left area of the square.

| Base | + ゃ | + ゅ | + ょ |
|---|---|---|---|
| き | きゃ kya | きゅ kyu | きょ kyo |
| し | しゃ sha | しゅ shu | しょ sho |
| ち | ちゃ cha | ちゅ chu | ちょ cho |
| に | にゃ nya | にゅ nyu | にょ nyo |
| ひ | ひゃ hya | ひゅ hyu | ひょ hyo |
| み | みゃ mya | みゅ myu | みょ myo |
| り | りゃ rya | りゅ ryu | りょ ryo |
| ぎ | ぎゃ gya | ぎゅ gyu | ぎょ gyo |
| じ | じゃ ja | じゅ ju | じょ jo |
| ぢ | ぢゃ ja (rare) | ぢゅ ju (rare) | ぢょ jo (rare) |
| び | びゃ bya | びゅ byu | びょ byo |
| ぴ | ぴゃ pya | ぴゅ pyu | ぴょ pyo |

Total: 36 combinations (21 unvoiced + 15 voiced/semi-voiced).

Big vs small matters: きよ (ki-yo, 2 beats) ≠ きょ (kyo, 1 beat). びよういん (beauty salon) ≠ びょういん (hospital).

## 4. Other Marks Used with Hiragana

| Mark | Name | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| っ (small tsu) | 促音 sokuon | Doubles the following consonant; occupies one full beat | きって kitte (stamp), がっこう gakkō (school) |
| ー | 長音符 chōonpu | Long-vowel bar — used in katakana; in hiragana long vowels are written with an extra vowel instead | おかあさん okāsan, おとうさん otōsan |
| ゝ | 踊り字 | Repetition mark, obsolete — you will only see it in old texts or names | — |

Long vowel spelling in hiragana:
- ā → add あ (おかあさん, おばあさん)
- ī → add い (おにいさん, おいしい)
- ū → add う (くうき, ゆうびん)
- ē → usually add い (せんせい, えいご, とけい); add え only in a few words (おねえさん, ええ)
- ō → usually add う (おとうさん, がっこう, きょう); add お in a small set of words (おおきい, とおい, とお, こおり, おおい, とおる)

## 5. Stroke-Order Tips (in words)

General rules that cover almost every character:

1. **Top before bottom.** Whatever is highest is written first (あ: the short horizontal stroke, then the vertical, then the loop).
2. **Left before right.** When strokes are side by side, write the left one first (い: left curve, then right dot; か: main hook first, then the short diagonal, then the right dot).
3. **Horizontal before vertical when they cross.** (き: two horizontals, then the vertical curve, then the bottom stroke. さ: horizontal, then the vertical curve, then the bottom.)
4. **Enclosing or main frame first, then what sits inside/attached.** (ほ: left vertical first, then the two horizontals and the loop on the right. は: same idea.)
5. **Vertical-through-centre strokes last.** (ま, も: the horizontals first, then the vertical that passes through — note も is the one exception: the vertical hook is written first, then the two horizontals.)
6. **Loops are drawn in one continuous motion** (あ, お, す, な, ぬ, ね, は, ほ, ま, む, よ, る). Never lift the pen inside a loop.
7. **Dots and dakuten/handakuten marks are written last**, after the main body, top-left to bottom-right.
8. **Small kana (ゃ ゅ ょ っ) follow the same order as their full-size versions**, just smaller and placed in the lower-right quadrant.

Stroke counts you should know (helps with recognising "how much ink" a character should have):
- 1 stroke: く, し, そ, つ, て, の, ひ, へ, る, ろ, ん
- 2 strokes: い, う, え, こ, す, ち, と, ぬ, ね, み, め, ゆ, よ, ら, り, れ, わ
- 3 strokes: あ, お, か, け, さ, せ, に, は, ま, む, も, や, を
- 4 strokes: き, た, な, ふ, ほ

(Printed and handwritten forms of さ, き, ふ, り differ slightly — in handwriting the last strokes of さ and き are usually disconnected. Both are correct.)

## 6. Ten Commonly Confused Pairs / Groups

| # | Pair | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **さ / き** | き has TWO horizontal strokes on top; さ has ONE. Think: き (ki) = key with 2 teeth. |
| 2 | **ぬ / め** | ぬ (nu) ends with a small loop at the bottom-right — "nu has a noodle knot". め (me) has no loop. |
| 3 | **わ / ね / れ** | All start with a vertical stroke. わ (wa) ends in a wide open curve; ね (ne) ends with a loop (like a knot on a net); れ (re) ends by flicking outward to the right without a loop. |
| 4 | **は / ほ** | ほ (ho) has an extra horizontal line on top of the right side; は (ha) has one horizontal only. |
| 5 | **る / ろ** | る (ru) has a loop at the bottom; ろ (ro) does not — "ru rolls up". |
| 6 | **あ / お** | お (o) has a separate dot at the top right; あ (a) does not. あ is more angular with a bigger loop. |
| 7 | **い / り** | い (i) has two short, separated, roughly equal strokes; り (ri) has a long right stroke sweeping down. |
| 8 | **こ / に** | に (ni) has a vertical stroke on the left; こ (ko) is just the two horizontals. |
| 9 | **ち / ら** | ち (chi) has a horizontal cross-bar and its curve opens to the left; ら (ra) starts with a short dot-like stroke and its curve opens to the right. |
| 10 | **す / む** | Both have a loop on a vertical stroke. す (su) has a single horizontal and a simple loop; む (mu) has a loop plus a small extra stroke/dot on the right. |

Bonus confusions to be aware of: つ / っ (size), ゆ / よ, け / は, う / ら, そ / ぞ (dakuten easy to miss), ば / ぱ (dakuten vs handakuten — check whether it is two strokes or a circle).
