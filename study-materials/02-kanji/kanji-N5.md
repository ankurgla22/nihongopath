# JLPT N5 Kanji — Complete List (103 Kanji, 10 Daily Sets)

This file covers all 103 kanji commonly tested at JLPT N5. Learn one "Day" set per day, review the previous days each morning, and you will have finished the list in under two weeks.

Reading conventions:
- **On-yomi** (Chinese-derived reading) is written in katakana.
- **Kun-yomi** (native Japanese reading) is written in hiragana. Okurigana (the part written in kana after the kanji) is shown after a dot, e.g. た.べる = 食べる.
- A dash (—) means that reading is not used at N5 level or does not exist.

---

## How to study kanji

**1. Learn radicals first.** Every kanji is built from a small set of components (radicals). 木 (tree) appears inside 休 (rest = person 亻 next to a tree), 校 (school), and 本 (book/origin = tree with a line at its root). When you can see 休 as "person + tree" instead of six random strokes, you remember it in a fraction of the time. Before each day's set, look at each kanji and ask "what pieces is this made of?" and invent a one-sentence story connecting the pieces to the meaning.

**2. Use spaced repetition (SRS).** Do not review everything every day. Use Anki (free) or a similar app with a deck that shows the kanji and asks for meaning + readings. Rate each card honestly. SRS will show hard cards often and easy cards rarely, which is the most time-efficient way to keep 100+ (later 1000+) kanji in memory. Add the example words as separate cards — readings are learned through words, not from isolated kanji.

**3. Recognition before writing.** The JLPT is a multiple-choice, reading-only test; you never write a single kanji. Prioritise: (a) recognise the kanji and know its meaning, (b) read it correctly in words, (c) write it by hand. Writing is still useful because it forces you to notice stroke details (e.g. 土 vs 士, 千 vs 干), so write each new kanji 5–10 times once, using correct stroke order (top to bottom, left to right, horizontal before vertical when they cross). After that, review by recognition only.

**4. Learn readings through words, not lists.** Most kanji have several readings; you only need the ones that appear in real N5 vocabulary. The "example words" column is the part to memorise. On-yomi is usually used in two-kanji compounds (学校 がっこう), kun-yomi when the kanji stands alone or has okurigana (学ぶ まなぶ).

**5. Daily routine (about 25 minutes).**
- 5 min: SRS review of all previous cards.
- 10 min: study today's 10 kanji — radicals, story, write each 5 times.
- 5 min: read the 20 example words aloud, cover the reading column, test yourself.
- 5 min: add today's cards to your SRS deck.
