# N2 読解 (Reading) — Strategies, Section Structure, and Weekly Routine

This guide covers the structure of the JLPT N2 reading section, how to allocate your time, the question types you will face and how to attack each one, the 20 discourse markers you must recognise instantly, and a weekly reading routine for the 6-month run-up to the exam.

---

## 1. Section structure

At N2, 言語知識（文字・語彙・文法）and 読解 are combined into **one 105-minute paper**. The reading portion is 問題10 to 問題14 (numbering may vary slightly by year), and accounts for roughly 60 of the 180 total points on the exam (読解 is scored as its own 60-point section, and you need at least 19/60 to avoid failing on the sectional minimum).

| 問題 | Name | Text length | Number of texts | Questions per text | Total questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 問題10 | 内容理解（短文） | ~200 characters | 5 | 1 | 5 |
| 問題11 | 内容理解（中文） | ~500 characters | 3 | 3 | 9 |
| 問題12 | 統合理解 | 2 texts, ~300 each (A/B) | 1 set | 2 | 2 |
| 問題13 | 主張理解（長文） | ~900 characters | 1 | 3 | 3 |
| 問題14 | 情報検索 | ~700 chars (notice, table, ad, schedule) | 1 | 2 | 2 |

**Total: 21 reading questions across 5 problem types.**

Texts are drawn from newspaper columns, essays, business emails, notices, explanatory writing, letters, and opinion pieces. Vocabulary and grammar are at N2 level; kanji are not glossed except for very rare readings.

### What each part tests

- **短文 (short passages)**: One question each. The question usually asks "what does the writer mean by ~", "what is this text trying to say", or "what is the purpose of this email/notice". Fast, precise reading.
- **中文 (medium passages)**: Three questions each. Typically one on a 指示語 or underlined phrase, one on a reason (なぜ〜か), and one on the writer's overall point.
- **統合理解 (integrated comprehension)**: Two texts (A and B) on the same topic by different writers. Questions ask what both agree on, what they differ on, or what one of them says that the other does not.
- **主張理解 (long passage, thesis comprehension)**: One long opinion essay. Questions test understanding of the writer's claim, the reasons for it, and specific details.
- **情報検索 (information retrieval)**: You do not read the whole thing. You are given a condition (e.g., "Ms. Tanaka wants to take a course on Saturday morning that costs under 5,000 yen") and must scan a table, notice, or advertisement to find the one option that fits.

## 2. Time allocation (105 minutes total for 言語知識 + 読解)

The single biggest cause of failure at N2 reading is running out of time. Plan the paper before you sit it.

| Block | Content | Target time | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|---|
| 問題1–6 | 文字・語彙 (漢字読み, 表記, 語形成, 文脈規定, 言い換え, 用法) | 15 min | 0:15 |
| 問題7–9 | 文法 (文の文法1, 文の文法2 並べ替え, 文章の文法) | 20 min | 0:35 |
| 問題10 | 短文 ×5 | 10 min (2 min each) | 0:45 |
| 問題11 | 中文 ×3 | 21 min (7 min each) | 1:06 |
| 問題12 | 統合理解 | 8 min | 1:14 |
| 問題13 | 主張理解（長文） | 12 min | 1:26 |
| 問題14 | 情報検索 | 7 min | 1:33 |
| Buffer | Re-check flagged answers, fill in blanks | 12 min | 1:45 |

### Rules for staying on schedule

1. **Write the clock targets on your question sheet** before starting (e.g., "10:35 → start 読解").
2. **Never leave a bubble blank.** There is no penalty for wrong answers. If time is running out, fill everything.
3. **Do 情報検索 (問題14) before 長文 (問題13)** if you are behind. 情報検索 is 2 questions for ~7 minutes of work and is the most predictable score in the section. 長文 is where people burn 20 minutes and still get 1/3.
4. **One pass per passage.** Do not go back and forth between passages. Answer, mark uncertain ones with a small dot, move on.
5. **Vocabulary/grammar must finish in 35 minutes.** If you are still on 文法 at 40 minutes, you are already sacrificing 読解 points. Practise the 言語知識 part with a timer every week.

## 3. Question types and how to attack each

### 3.1 Read the question first — always

Before reading any passage, read the question stem(s), and for 短文 also skim the four options. This tells you what to look for and primes you to notice the relevant sentence. It converts "reading" into "searching", which is far faster.

For 中文 and 長文, read all three question stems first, note any underlined phrases they refer to, and then read the passage in one pass, pausing at each underlined section to answer that question before moving on.

### 3.2 Finding 筆者の主張 (the writer's claim)

The writer's main point is almost never in the first sentence. Japanese expository prose typically:

1. Opens with a general observation, anecdote, or commonly held view (一般論).
2. Pivots with a contrastive marker (しかし, ところが, だが, とはいえ).
3. States the writer's own view after the pivot.
4. Supports it with reasons/examples (なぜなら, 例えば, というのは).
5. Restates the conclusion near the end (つまり, 要するに, したがって, このように).

**Where to look:**
- The sentence immediately after しかし / ところが / だが in the first half.
- The final two or three sentences of the passage, especially after つまり / 要するに / このように / 〜のではないだろうか.
- Sentence endings that express the writer's stance: 〜べきだ, 〜と思う, 〜のではないか, 〜と言えるだろう, 〜必要がある, 〜てはならない, 〜に違いない, 〜ことが大切だ, 〜と考えている.

**Trap to avoid:** Options that quote the 一般論 (the view the writer introduces only to disagree with). The passage will *contain* those words, which makes the option look correct. Ask: "Is this what the writer believes, or what the writer says *other people* believe?"

**Watch for rhetorical negatives:** 〜のではないだろうか means the writer *does* think so. 〜とは限らない / 〜わけではない mean partial denial, not full denial. 〜ないことはない means "it is possible / it does happen".

### 3.3 Handling 指示語 (これ / それ / この / その / そうした / このような)

A very common question: 「『それ』は何を指しているか」 or 「『このこと』とは何か」.

**Method:**
1. Locate the 指示語 in the text.
2. Look **backwards** — the referent is almost always in the preceding one or two sentences. (Rarely, これ / 次のような refers forward, usually before a colon or list.)
3. Find the noun or the clause that can be substituted for the 指示語 so the sentence still makes sense.
4. Substitute it in and re-read. If the sentence is now natural, that is the referent.
5. Compare with the options. The correct option often paraphrases the referent rather than quoting it word for word.

**Nuances:**
- そ- series (それ, その, そうした) usually points to something already mentioned that is slightly "distant" — often the *other person's* idea or the previous sentence's main noun.
- こ- series (これ, この, こうした) often points to what was just said or what the writer is focused on now.
- あ- series (あれ, あの) in essays refers to shared, distant knowledge; rare in exam questions.
- 「このような〜」「そうした〜」 usually summarise a whole preceding situation, not a single noun. The correct answer will be a clause-length paraphrase.

### 3.4 Tracking 接続詞 (connectives)

Connectives are the skeleton of the argument. Every time you meet one, register what it does:

| Function | Markers | What to expect next |
|---|---|---|
| Reversal (逆接) | しかし, ところが, だが, とはいえ, けれども | Writer's real view, or a surprising fact |
| Cause → result (順接) | したがって, そこで, だから, そのため | Conclusion / action taken |
| Restatement (換言) | つまり, すなわち, 要するに, 言い換えれば | Summary — very likely the answer to a 主張 question |
| Addition (添加) | さらに, また, しかも, それに | Additional evidence — same direction as before |
| Example (例示) | 例えば, 〜など | Illustration — supports, but is not itself the claim |
| Reason (理由) | なぜなら, というのは, 〜からだ | Answer to a なぜ question |
| Contrast (対比) | 一方, それに対して, 逆に | Two sides being compared — key in 統合理解 |
| Condition/limit (補足) | ただし, もっとも, なお | Exception or caveat — often tested in 情報検索 |

Circle contrastive and restatement markers as you read. When a question asks for the writer's opinion, look at what follows the circled しかし and つまり.

### 3.5 Negative-question traps

Some questions are phrased in the negative:

- 「本文の内容と**合わないもの**はどれか」 (Which does NOT match the text?)
- 「筆者が述べて**いない**ことはどれか」
- 「〜の理由として**正しくないもの**はどれか」

**Attack method:**
1. Underline 合わない / いない / ない / 正しくない in the question stem. Physically mark it.
2. Go through options one by one and mark each ○ (matches text) or × (does not match).
3. The answer is the single ×.
4. Watch for options that are *true in the real world* but *not stated in the passage* — these are "not in the text", so they count as 合わない.

Under time pressure, people read a negative question as a positive one and confidently pick the first option that matches the text. This is a pure attention error, so mark the stem every time.

### 3.6 「最も適切なもの」 / 「最もよく合うもの」 — choosing the best of several plausible options

When the stem says "most appropriate", typically two options are clearly wrong and two are close. Distinguish the two close options by:

- **Scope**: Is one option too broad (claims something about "all people" when the text says "many young people") or too narrow (refers only to one example rather than the point)?
- **Degree**: 必ず vs たいてい, 全く〜ない vs あまり〜ない. The text's hedging must match the option's hedging.
- **Subject**: Does the option attribute the view to the right person (writer vs quoted expert vs "people in general")?
- **Causality direction**: AだからB vs BだからA. Options often reverse cause and effect.
- **Added content**: An option that adds a claim the text never makes (e.g., "and this should be made law") is wrong even if the first half is right.

The correct option is usually a **paraphrase** — synonyms, different grammar, same meaning. The distractors often **copy exact words** from the text but twist the meaning. If an option looks *too* lifted from the passage, be suspicious.

### 3.7 Reason questions (なぜ〜か / 〜のはどうしてか)

1. Find the sentence with the underlined phrase.
2. Look for explicit reason markers nearby: なぜなら, というのは, 〜からだ, 〜ためだ, 〜ので, 〜によって.
3. If there is no explicit marker, the reason is usually in the sentence *before* (the situation that led to it) or *after* (an explanation starting with something like それは〜).
4. Check the option that best matches the causal chain. Beware options that give a *result* rather than a *reason*.

### 3.8 統合理解 (A/B texts)

Typical questions:
- 「AとBで共通して述べられていることは何か」 (What do both A and B say?)
- 「AとBは〜についてどのように述べているか」 (How do A and B each view ~?)
- 「Aの筆者とBの筆者の考えとして正しいものはどれか」

**Method:**
1. Read the questions first so you know whether you need agreement, difference, or each writer's stance.
2. Read A. In the margin write in 3–5 words: A's stance (賛成/反対/条件付き) and A's main reason.
3. Read B. Same.
4. For "common point" questions, the answer is usually a general premise both accept (e.g., "the problem exists", "it depends on how it is used") rather than the conclusion — because the conclusions usually differ.
5. For "difference" questions, the option must state *both* positions correctly. Check each half separately; distractors typically get A right and B wrong.

### 3.9 情報検索 — scanning notices, ads, and tables

You are not meant to read everything. The question gives you a person and their conditions.

**Method:**
1. Read the question. List the conditions explicitly (e.g., ① Sunday, ② beginner, ③ under 3,000 yen, ④ wants a certificate).
2. Look at the document's structure: headings, table columns, footnotes marked ※ or 注.
3. Eliminate rows/options by one condition at a time. Cross them out physically.
4. **Read the fine print (※, ただし, なお, 注意)**. The exam almost always hides a decisive condition in a note: a cancellation fee, an age limit, an "excluding holidays", "students only with ID", "apply by the 10th", "materials fee extra".
5. Numbers: check dates, times, prices, and deadlines carefully. Note 以上 (≥), 以下 (≤), 未満 (<), 〜を超える (>), 〜まで (until, inclusive), 〜前 (before). 「20歳以上」 includes 20; 「20歳未満」 does not.
6. Common second-question patterns: "How much in total does this person pay?" (add the fee + material cost − discount), "By when must they apply?", "What must they bring?".

Do this section in under 7 minutes; it is the most reliable 2 points in the paper.

### 3.10 Reading the options

- Read all four options before deciding, even if option 1 looks right.
- Cross out options that contain an outright contradiction with the text.
- Cross out options that add information not in the text.
- Between the remaining, pick the one that matches the *writer's stance* and the *scope* of the text.

## 4. Twenty discourse markers you must recognise instantly

| # | Marker | Reading | Meaning / function | What follows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | しかし | しかし | "However." Standard reversal. | Writer's contrasting point, often the real claim. |
| 2 | つまり | つまり | "In other words / that is to say." Restatement. | Clear summary of the previous point — answer material. |
| 3 | ところが | ところが | "But, contrary to expectation." Stronger surprise than しかし. | An unexpected fact or turn of events. |
| 4 | したがって | したがって | "Therefore / accordingly." Logical conclusion. | The conclusion drawn from what precedes. |
| 5 | むしろ | むしろ | "Rather / if anything." Corrects a comparison. | "Not A; if anything, B." B is the writer's preference. |
| 6 | 一方 | いっぽう | "On the other hand / meanwhile." Sets up a parallel contrast. | The second of two compared items or groups. |
| 7 | ただし | ただし | "However, note that / provided that." Adds a condition or exception. | A restriction — critical in 情報検索 and rule texts. |
| 8 | なぜなら | なぜなら | "Because / the reason is." Introduces reason; sentence ends 〜からだ. | The reason for the previous claim. |
| 9 | すなわち | すなわち | "Namely / that is." Formal restatement or definition. | Precise re-expression or definition. |
| 10 | とはいえ | とはいえ | "That said / even so." Concedes the previous point, then qualifies. | A limitation on what was just admitted. |
| 11 | 要するに | ようするに | "In short / to sum up." Compresses a long argument. | The core message — look here for 主張. |
| 12 | 例えば | たとえば | "For example." Illustration. | A concrete case supporting the previous general claim. |
| 13 | さらに | さらに | "Furthermore / in addition." Escalating addition. | More evidence in the same direction, often stronger. |
| 14 | また | また | "Also / and." Neutral addition or parallel point. | A second item in a list of points. |
| 15 | そこで | そこで | "So / therefore (as a next step)." Situation → action. | An action or decision taken in response to the situation. |
| 16 | それに対して | それにたいして | "In contrast to that." Explicit contrast with the preceding item. | The opposing side of a comparison. |
| 17 | というのは | というのは | "The thing is / this is because." Explains a preceding statement. | An explanation or reason; sentence often ends 〜からだ / 〜のだ. |
| 18 | 確かに〜。しかし〜 | たしかに〜しかし | "Certainly ~. However ~." Concession then reversal. | 確かに part = commonly accepted view (not the writer's). しかし part = writer's real view. |
| 19 | もちろん〜が | もちろん〜が | "Of course ~, but ~." Same concession pattern in one sentence. | The clause after が is the writer's point. |
| 20 | いずれにしても | いずれにしても | "In any case / either way." Sets aside the debate and states what holds regardless. | The conclusion that stands regardless of the preceding alternatives — often the final claim. |

### Additional markers worth knowing (bonus)

- だが — formal しかし.
- もっとも — "though / mind you" (a softening qualification).
- なお — "additionally / note that" (in notices).
- ちなみに — "incidentally" (aside; rarely the main point).
- 逆に — "conversely".
- そのため — "for that reason".
- このように — "in this way / as shown above" (summary — 主張 signal).
- 〜に過ぎない — "is nothing more than" (dismissive evaluation).
- 〜ものの — "although" (concession inside a sentence).
- 〜どころか — "far from ~ / let alone".

## 5. Sentence-ending signals of stance

The ending of a sentence tells you whether the writer is stating fact, opinion, hearsay, or doubt. Train yourself to notice these:

| Ending | Signal |
|---|---|
| 〜べきだ / 〜べきではない | Strong opinion — writer's claim |
| 〜必要がある / 〜なければならない | Recommendation — writer's claim |
| 〜のではないだろうか / 〜のではないか | Rhetorical — writer affirms this |
| 〜と思う / 〜と考える / 〜と言えるだろう | Opinion |
| 〜そうだ / 〜という / 〜と言われている | Hearsay — not the writer's own view |
| 〜かもしれない | Tentative — weak claim |
| 〜に違いない | Strong inference |
| 〜わけではない / 〜とは限らない | Partial denial — the writer is refining, not rejecting |
| 〜ざるを得ない | "Cannot help but" — reluctant conclusion |
| 〜てほしい / 〜てもらいたい | Wish addressed to the reader or society |

## 6. Weekly reading routine (6 months)

Reading speed and stamina take months to build. Do reading **every day**, not only on weekends. Total target: about 6–7 hours of reading per week, alongside vocabulary and grammar work.

### Daily core (Monday–Friday, 45–60 minutes)

| Day | Task | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | 短文 ×3 timed (2 min each), then review: identify why each distractor is wrong. Read one NHK NEWS WEB EASY article → then the normal NHK version of the same story. | 45 min |
| Tue | 中文 ×1 timed (7 min), full review. Rewrite the passage's main claim in one Japanese sentence. Note 5 new words. | 45 min |
| Wed | Extensive reading: 20 minutes of a graded reader, novel, or column (no dictionary, no stopping). Then 10 minutes summarising in Japanese. | 45 min |
| Thu | 中文 ×1 or 統合理解 ×1 timed. Highlight every connective and 指示語; write what each refers to. | 50 min |
| Fri | 情報検索 ×1 timed (7 min) + 短文 ×2. Then 20 minutes of newspaper column reading (天声人語-style, 社説, or a コラム). | 50 min |

### Weekend

| Day | Task | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Sat | Full 読解 set under exam timing: 5 短文, 3 中文, 1 統合理解, 1 長文, 1 情報検索 (~58 min). Then a 40-minute review: for every wrong or guessed answer, write in your error log (a) the question type, (b) why the wrong option was tempting, (c) what clue you missed. | 100 min |
| Sun | Error-log review (20 min). Re-read two passages from the week aloud (fluency and speed). Extensive reading 30 minutes. | 60 min |

### Phase plan across the 6 months

| Month | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | N3-level passages until 90%+ accuracy. Learn all 20 markers cold. Build the habit of reading the question first. Daily NHK EASY. |
| 2 | Move to N2 短文 and 中文. Start the error log. Timed practice from week 5. |
| 3 | Add 統合理解 and 情報検索. Begin one full 読解 set every Saturday. Start reading untimed newspaper columns daily. |
| 4 | Add 長文. Push reading speed: aim for 中文 in 6 minutes. Read 2 columns a day. |
| 5 | Full 言語知識 + 読解 paper every Saturday (105 min). Analyse time per part. Fix the slowest part. |
| 6 | Two full mock exams per week in the final 3 weeks. Taper new material; review error log weekly. Final week: light reading, sleep, no new vocab. |

### Measuring progress

Every 4 weeks, record:
- Accuracy on each 問題 type (aim: 短文 80%, 中文 75%, 統合 70%, 長文 65%, 情報検索 95%).
- Time per 中文 passage (aim: ≤7 min by month 4, ≤6 min by month 6).
- Words per minute on an untimed N2 essay (aim: 250+ characters per minute by exam time).

### Materials

- Official JLPT sample questions and past papers (日本語能力試験 公式問題集 N2, two volumes) — the gold standard for difficulty calibration.
- 新完全マスター 読解 N2 — best for strategy drills per question type.
- 日本語総まとめ N2 読解 — good daily short exercises.
- NHK NEWS WEB EASY → NHK ordinary news — pair the two to bridge N3 → N2 vocabulary.
- Newspaper columns (朝日 天声人語, 毎日 余録, 読売 編集手帳) — ~600 characters, ideal 中文 practice once you reach month 3.

## 7. One-page checklist to keep beside you while practising

1. Read the question stem(s) first. Underline negative words (合わない, いない, 正しくない).
2. Circle every しかし / ところが / つまり / 要するに / 一方 / ただし as you read.
3. For 指示語 questions, look backwards, substitute, re-read.
4. For 主張 questions, check what follows the pivot and the last three sentences.
5. Distrust options that copy exact wording; trust paraphrases.
6. Check scope, degree, subject, and causal direction on the two closest options.
7. In 情報検索, list conditions, eliminate by row, read every ※ note.
8. Never spend more than the time budget on one passage. Mark, move on, return only in the buffer.
9. Fill every bubble.
10. Log every error with its cause.
